As a sculptor working within a Graphic Design course I generally felt very intimidated and out of my depth at CSM.When I first met Phil it was through being set the impossible task of ‘helping’ him to organise and clear parts of his cave of Type at the college. I very quickly learnt that my endeavours were not appreciated and I was not welcome. However, maybe as a distraction technique, he learnt that my efforts would be slowed or halted entirely if he told me about the way different letter forms from the collection were made and the history and evolution of sign manufacturing. His sheer excitement for letters was infectious and in the end we compromised: clearing some of the signs and lettering out of the space - so long as he could then satirically arrange them throughout the studios and college.

Phil made type captivating and fun in a way I never anticipated or even thought possible. His love and passion for the medium shined through so intensely it changed my whole practice.

Ta Mr Baines, It was an absolute privilege to work with you at the University O farts London.

Mike McShane

I was working with Phil on famous Beatles illustrator Alan Aldridge's "Kaleidoscope Eyes" biography. Phil wasn't happy with the quality of the photographs of Alan's work, so we had the original illustrations shipped over from the States. It was a nail-biting affair, as they were all priceless, of course. After they arrived, Phil looked at them, all sitting on a desk in a neat stack, and said with his cheeky smile: "You know Max, we could just sell them all and never work again. We'd have to flee the country, though. You in?"

What I loved most about Phil was that he was utterly professional and painfully detail-obsessed, but paired it all effortlessly with warm humanity, and a witty humour that made work always feel more like play.

Play on, Phil, wherever you are.

Max Ackermann

A portrait of Phil from a shoot during my second year at Central Saint Martins, Long Acre—when I photographed all of the tutors and technicians with a memorable object. Phil arrived with his letter set. As an image maker (I spent most of my time in the darkroom) I wasn’t particularly well versed in typography.

Somewhat ambitiously, I decided to design and typeset my dissertation as a book… with what can only be described as a saintly patience, Phil marked up my entire document with his red pen (there was a lot) and initiated me in the joys of typography… em dashes and all.

It was many years after graduating that our paths crossed again, in the context of Boyle&Perks when we spoke with him for advice on creating CSM SHIFTS.

He reminded me it needed to be anarchic… which was one of the key drivers in making the headline font randomised.

I feel lucky to have had such encounters with a very special tutor.
